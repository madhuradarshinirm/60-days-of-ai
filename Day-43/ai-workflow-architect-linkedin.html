STAGE 01 / 07

Strategy & Positioning

⏱ 3–5 hrs (one-time) 🎯 Foundation stage
Objectives
  • Define a niche narrow enough to be memorable, wide enough to sustain a year of posts
  • Identify your ideal reader and the one transformation your content offers them
  • Audit 5–10 competitors/peers to find a gap you can own
Tasks
  • Write a one-line positioning statement (role + audience + outcome)
  • List 3 content pillars that support that positioning
  • Rewrite headline, About section, and featured section around the new angle
  • Build a swipe file of 15 posts (in and out of niche) that resonate with you
Best AI Tools
Claude
Strongest at open-ended strategic reasoning — good for stress-testing a positioning statement against your actual experience and pointing out where it's generic.
Alternatives: ChatGPT (GPT-5), Gemini
Perplexity
Fast, cited web research for competitor and market scans — good for pulling recent examples rather than stale training data.
Alternatives: ChatGPT with browsing, Google Gemini
Shield Analytics
Purpose-built LinkedIn analytics for benchmarking peers' post cadence and format mix before you commit to pillars.
Alternatives: Favikon, Taplio's analytics view
Prompt Examples
I'm a [role] with [X years] experience in [industry]. Interview me with 8 questions to uncover a specific, differentiated LinkedIn niche — avoid generic advice, push back on anything that sounds like every other post in this space.
Here are 10 LinkedIn posts I admire [paste text]. Identify the underlying pattern in hook, structure, and voice across them, and tell me which patterns I could credibly adapt given my background in [X].
Best Practices
  • Pick pillars you can talk about for a year without repeating yourself
  • Anchor positioning in real experience — AI can sharpen it, not invent it
  • Write the positioning statement down somewhere you'll actually revisit
Common Mistakes
  • Choosing a niche identical to five competitors' — no wedge
  • Positioning around a title ("marketer") instead of a point of view
  • Skipping this stage and going straight to posting
Expected Outputs

A one-line positioning statement, 3 documented content pillars, an updated profile (headline/About/Featured), and a swipe file.

Efficiency Tip

Do this once well — a shaky foundation forces you to redo profile copy and pillar decisions every few months, which costs far more time than getting it right up front.

STAGE 02 / 07

Content Pillars & Calendar

⏱ 2–3 hrs (monthly refresh)🎯 Planning stage
Objectives
  • Convert 3 pillars into a running bank of specific post ideas
  • Balance formats: text posts, carousels, polls, video, comments-as-content
  • Set a sustainable cadence (most successful thought-leaders post 3–5x/week)
Tasks
  • Generate 30 post ideas per pillar per quarter
  • Slot ideas into a 4-week rolling calendar
  • Tag each idea with format and a rough hook angle
  • Reserve 20% of slots for reactive/trending topics
Best AI Tools
ChatGPT (GPT-5)
Fast at bulk ideation and turning a pillar into a long list of angles without repeating itself as much as smaller models.
Alternatives: Claude, Gemini
Notion AI
Ideas and calendar live in the same workspace — database views turn a flat idea list into a filterable content calendar.
Alternatives: Airtable + AI extension, Trello
Taplio
LinkedIn-specific idea generator and scheduler that shows what's currently performing in your niche while you plan.
Alternatives: Supergrow, AuthoredUp
Prompt Examples
My LinkedIn pillars are: [pillar 1], [pillar 2], [pillar 3]. Generate 15 specific post ideas per pillar — each one a concrete claim, story, or question, not a vague topic. Mark which would work best as a text post vs a carousel.
Turn this list of 20 raw post ideas [paste] into a 4-week calendar, 4 posts/week, alternating formats, with no two similar ideas within 5 days of each other.
Best Practices
  • Batch-generate ideas monthly, not post-by-post — protects momentum
  • Keep a running "idea inbox" for things you notice during the week
  • Vary hook type (contrarian, story, data, question) across the week
Common Mistakes
  • Over-planning 3 months out, then abandoning it when reality shifts
  • Every idea sounding like generic "5 tips" listicle content
  • No room in the calendar for timely/reactive posts
Expected Outputs

A rolling idea bank (60–90 ideas), a 4-week content calendar with format tags, and a lightweight weekly review ritual.

Efficiency Tip

Generate ideas in one big monthly batch instead of daily — batching cuts context-switching and produces more original angles than one-off requests.

STAGE 03 / 07

Writing & Drafting

⏱ 20–40 min / post🎯 Execution stage
Objectives
  • Turn a one-line idea into a full draft with a strong hook and clear payoff
  • Preserve a recognizable, human voice — not detectable AI phrasing
  • Produce drafts fast enough to sustain the calendar's cadence
Tasks
  • Draft the hook (first 1–2 lines) separately, test 3 variants
  • Write the body: story/argument/data, then the payoff or CTA
  • Run it through a voice pass so it matches your swipe-file style
  • Trim to the length the idea actually needs — no padding
Best AI Tools
Claude
Best-in-class at holding a specific voice across a draft when fed a few reference posts — less prone to generic "AI-speak" than most alternatives.
Alternatives: ChatGPT (GPT-5), Jasper
AuthoredUp
Writes and formats directly for LinkedIn's line-break and preview rules, so what you draft matches what readers will actually see.
Alternatives: Supergrow editor, Taplio editor
Grammarly
Fast final-pass proofing for tone and clarity without rewriting your voice, unlike a full AI rewrite.
Alternatives: ProWritingAid, Hemingway Editor
Prompt Examples
Here are 5 posts in my voice [paste]. Using that voice, draft a LinkedIn post from this idea: [idea]. Hook must earn a click on "see more" in the first line. No em-dash-heavy AI cadence, no generic conclusions.
Give me 5 alternative hooks (first 1-2 lines only) for this post idea: [idea]. Vary the approach: one contrarian, one story-based, one data-led, one question, one bold claim.
Best Practices
  • Always feed the model real examples of your voice — never write from a blank prompt
  • Write the hook last, after you know what the post actually proves
  • Edit AI output by hand — treat it as a first draft, not a final one
Common Mistakes
  • Publishing raw AI output without a human edit pass
  • Burying the point in paragraph 4 instead of the hook
  • Letting every post default to the same "3 lessons I learned" template
Expected Outputs

A polished draft per calendar slot, formatted for LinkedIn's line breaks, in your own edited voice.

Efficiency Tip

Draft a week of posts in one sitting using the same voice-reference prompt — reusing context is faster than re-explaining your voice every time.

STAGE 04 / 07

Visual Production

⏱ 30–60 min / asset🎯 Execution stage
Objectives
  • Build a lightweight visual system so posts are recognizable at a glance
  • Turn high-value text posts into carousels for reach and saves
  • Produce short video/talking-head content for a fuller format mix
Tasks
  • Design a simple carousel template (font, colors, layout) once, reuse it
  • Convert your top-performing text posts into 6–10 slide carousels
  • Batch-record short vertical video clips monthly
  • Create 2–3 reusable graphic formats (quote card, stat card, framework diagram)
Best AI Tools
Canva (Magic Design)
Fastest way to apply a consistent template to a batch of carousels without a design background.
Alternatives: Figma + plugins, Pitch
Claude / ChatGPT
Best for turning a finished post into a slide-by-slide carousel outline (one idea per slide, clear slide-to-slide flow) before you touch a design tool.
Alternatives: Gemini
Descript
Edits talking-head video by editing a transcript, and auto-generates captions — meaningfully faster than timeline-based editors for short clips.
Alternatives: CapCut, OpusClip (for repurposing longer video)
Prompt Examples
Turn this LinkedIn post [paste] into a 8-slide carousel outline. One idea per slide, slide 1 is the hook, slide 8 is a CTA. Keep each slide under 25 words.
Write a 60-second talking-head video script from this post idea [idea], structured for someone speaking straight to camera with no visual aids — natural spoken rhythm, not written prose.
Best Practices
  • Lock a template early — visual consistency builds recognition faster than novelty
  • Reserve carousels for posts that already proved themselves as text
  • Keep on-slide text minimal; let the caption carry nuance
Common Mistakes
  • Changing the visual template every few posts — no brand recall
  • Overcrowding slides with text nobody will read on mobile
  • Treating video as an afterthought instead of a monthly batch
Expected Outputs

A reusable carousel template, 2–4 carousels/month, and 2–4 short video clips/month, all on-brand.

Efficiency Tip

Batch-produce visuals monthly against the calendar from Stage 2, rather than designing reactively the day a post goes out.

STAGE 05 / 07

Publishing & Optimization

⏱ 10–15 min / post🎯 Execution stage
Objectives
  • Publish at times your specific audience is actually active
  • Format each post so the "see more" fold doesn't kill it
  • Seed early engagement in the critical first 60–90 minutes
Tasks
  • Check your own analytics for best posting windows (not generic advice)
  • Format with intentional line breaks, no walls of text
  • Add one thoughtful comment yourself right after publishing to kick off the thread
  • Alert 3–5 close connections when something particularly matters
Best AI Tools
Taplio
Schedules posts at data-backed optimal times for your specific audience and previews exactly where the "see more" cut lands.
Alternatives: Supergrow, Buffer
AuthoredUp
Shows a live preview of line breaks and the fold exactly as LinkedIn will render them — avoids awkward mid-sentence cuts.
Alternatives: Shield's post preview tool
Claude / ChatGPT
Good for a final "does this hook survive the fold" check and tightening the first two lines before scheduling.
Alternatives: Gemini
Prompt Examples
Here's my draft post [paste]. The first 2 lines are all that shows before "see more" on mobile. Rewrite just those 2 lines to be a standalone hook that creates a genuine reason to click, without misleading clickbait.
Suggest 3 short, specific first-comment questions I could post myself right after publishing this to invite genuine responses: [paste post].
Best Practices
  • Post consistently at 2–3 tested times rather than chasing "best time" advice online
  • Stay near your device for the first hour to reply to early comments quickly
  • Avoid external links in the main post body; put them in the first comment
Common Mistakes
  • Scheduling and walking away — missing the critical early-engagement window
  • Weak first two lines that don't survive the "see more" fold
  • Posting at generic "9am Tuesday" advice instead of your audience's actual activity
Expected Outputs

A published, well-formatted post with a survived hook, an early self-comment, and a documented best-time slot.

Efficiency Tip

Schedule a week of posts in one sitting on Sunday, then block 60–90 minutes after each goes live purely for engagement — don't multitask that window.

STAGE 06 / 07

Engagement & Network

⏱ 20–30 min / day🎯 Compounding stage
Objectives
  • Grow reach through genuine engagement, not vanity commenting
  • Turn commenters and DMs into a recognizable community over time
  • Build relationships with a handful of peers whose audiences overlap with yours
Tasks
  • Reply substantively to every comment on your own posts
  • Leave 5–10 genuine comments daily on posts in your niche
  • Track and follow up on meaningful DMs
  • Identify 10–15 peer accounts for regular, real engagement
Best AI Tools
Claude / ChatGPT
Useful for drafting a thoughtful comment starting point on complex posts, especially outside your core expertise — always edit before posting.
Alternatives: Gemini
Shield Analytics
Surfaces which of your comments actually drove profile visits, helping you focus engagement effort where it compounds.
Alternatives: Taplio's engagement tracker
Notion / Airtable
A simple CRM-style tracker for meaningful connections and DM follow-ups, so relationship-building doesn't rely on memory.
Alternatives: Folk, a plain spreadsheet
Prompt Examples
Here's a post from someone in my niche [paste]. Draft a genuine, specific comment that adds a perspective or example from my experience in [X] — not generic praise, no more than 3 sentences.
Someone DM'd me: [paste]. Draft a warm, specific reply that continues the conversation and offers one genuinely useful next step, without being salesy.
Best Practices
  • Always personalize AI-drafted comments before posting — copy-paste is detectable
  • Prioritize replying to your own post's comments over commenting elsewhere
  • Engage consistently, not in bursts — algorithms and relationships both reward steadiness
Common Mistakes
  • Posting AI comments verbatim across dozens of threads — reads as spam
  • Only engaging with big accounts, ignoring your own commenters
  • Treating engagement as a numbers game instead of relationship-building
Expected Outputs

A daily engagement habit, a tracked list of meaningful connections, and a growing set of peer relationships in your niche.

Efficiency Tip

Time-box engagement to a fixed 20–30 minute window daily — open-ended scrolling for "engagement" is the single biggest time sink in this workflow.

STAGE 07 / 07

Analytics & Iteration

⏱ 1 hr / week, 2 hrs / month🎯 Feedback loop
Objectives
  • Identify which pillars, formats, and hooks actually perform for your audience
  • Feed findings back into Stage 1–2 so the strategy keeps sharpening
  • Track profile-level growth, not just single-post metrics
Tasks
  • Weekly: review impressions, engagement rate, and comments-per-post by pillar
  • Monthly: identify top 3 and bottom 3 posts and diagnose why
  • Quarterly: revisit positioning and pillars against what's actually resonating
  • Track follower quality (relevant profiles) not just follower count
Best AI Tools
Shield Analytics
The most complete LinkedIn-native analytics layer — breaks down performance by pillar, format, and posting time beyond native LinkedIn data.
Alternatives: Favikon, native LinkedIn analytics
Claude / ChatGPT (Data Analysis)
Good at spotting patterns across a month of exported post data that are easy to miss scanning a spreadsheet by eye.
Alternatives: Gemini, Julius AI
Google Sheets + AI extensions
Free, flexible way to keep a running post-performance log once you outgrow relying on a single tool's dashboard.
Alternatives: Notion database, Airtable
Prompt Examples
Here is a month of my post data (topic, format, hook type, impressions, engagement rate): [paste table]. Identify the 3 clearest patterns in what performed well vs poorly, and suggest 2 concrete changes for next month.
My top 3 posts this quarter were about [X, Y, Z] and my bottom 3 were about [A, B, C]. Given my original positioning statement [paste], should I adjust my pillars? Argue both sides before recommending.
Best Practices
  • Judge performance against your own baseline, not viral outliers from strangers
  • Weight comments and saves higher than raw likes — they signal real resonance
  • Give a new pillar or format at least 4–6 posts before judging it
Common Mistakes
  • Reacting to a single underperforming post by abandoning the pillar entirely
  • Only tracking vanity metrics (likes) instead of profile visits and DMs
  • Never closing the loop back to Stage 1 positioning
Expected Outputs

A weekly performance snapshot, a monthly pattern report, and a quarterly positioning review that updates Stage 1–2.

Efficiency Tip

Keep one running spreadsheet of every post's core stats instead of re-deriving insights from scratch each month — pattern recognition compounds with more history.

CONCLUSION

Workflow Summary

This workflow moves from a one-time strategic foundation (positioning, pillars) into a repeating monthly-to-weekly production loop: plan → write → design → publish → engage → measure → refine. The loop compounds because Stage 7's analytics feed directly back into Stage 1's positioning and Stage 2's pillar mix, so each quarter's content should get sharper than the last. Most of the time cost sits in Stages 3–6 (writing, design, publishing, engagement); Stages 1 and 7 are lower-frequency but highest-leverage — get those right and the rest gets easier.
